### Diagnostic Criteria and Imaging Studies for Blurred Vision and Confusion
- **Diabetic Retinopathy**:
  - **Criteria**: Diagnosis typically involves a comprehensive eye examination, including fundoscopic examination to assess for retinal changes.
  - **Imaging**: Optical coherence tomography (OCT) can help visualize retinal layers and detect edema or other changes indicative of diabetic retinopathy.
  
- **Neurological Conditions (Dementia or Stroke)**:
  - **Imaging Studies**: 
    - **CT or MRI of the brain**: Essential for identifying structural changes, ischemic areas, or hemorrhages that could explain confusion and visual disturbances.
    - **Neurological Assessment**: A thorough clinical evaluation to assess cognitive function and neurological signs.

### Laboratory Tests for Tingling in Hands and Feet
- **Vitamin B12 Levels**: 
  - Important to rule out deficiency, which can cause neuropathy and cognitive issues.
  
- **Glucose Levels**: 
  - To assess for diabetes control, as fluctuations can lead to neuropathic symptoms.
  
- **Thyroid Function Tests**: 
  - Hypothyroidism can contribute to peripheral neuropathy and cognitive decline; testing TSH and free T4 is recommended.

### Non-Pharmacological Interventions for Cognitive Function
- **Cognitive Therapy**: 
  - Tailored cognitive rehabilitation strategies can help improve memory and problem-solving skills.
  
- **Social Engagement Strategies**: 
  - Encouraging participation in group activities or community programs can enhance social interaction and cognitive stimulation.
  
- **Structured Routine**: 
  - Implementing a daily schedule can help improve orientation and reduce confusion.

### Medication Adjustments for Confusion Management
- **Review Current Medications**: 
  - Assess for potential interactions, especially with medications that may exacerbate confusion (e.g., anticholinergics).
  
- **Consider Alternatives**: 
  - If the patient is on medications that may contribute to cognitive impairment, such as certain sedatives or opioids, consider tapering or switching to alternatives with a better cognitive profile.
  
- **Monitor for Side Effects**: 
  - Regularly evaluate the patient for side effects from current medications, particularly those affecting cognitive function, and adjust as necessary. 

By addressing these areas, a comprehensive approach can be taken to manage the patient's symptoms effectively while investigating underlying causes.
