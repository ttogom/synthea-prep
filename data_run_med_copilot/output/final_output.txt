## Plan:

1. **Neurological and Cognitive Assessment**
   - Refer to a neurologist for an evaluation of potential neurological conditions.
   - Schedule a comprehensive eye examination with an ophthalmologist to assess the cause of blurred vision.
   - Conduct formal cognitive testing using MMSE or MoCA to evaluate cognitive function.
   - Consider a neuropsychological evaluation if preliminary assessments indicate significant cognitive impairment.

2. **Medications**
   - **Vitamin B12 Supplementation**: Initiate if deficiency is suspected, as it may alleviate tingling in hands and feet.
   - Consider starting medications for neuropathic symptoms such as gabapentin or pregabalin if tingling persists.

3. **Management of Underlying Conditions**
   - Monitor and optimize diabetes management through adjustments to metformin dosage if necessary.
   - Evaluate current blood pressure management while on lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide to ensure optimal control, considering the potential impact on cognitive function.

4. **Social Support and Stress Management**
   - Encourage the patient to engage in social activities or support groups to reduce feelings of isolation and enhance social interaction.
   - Introduce mindfulness practices (e.g., meditation or yoga) to help lower stress levels.
   - Refer to a psychologist or counselor for cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) to address anxiety and confusion related to cognitive decline.

5. **Patient and Family Education**
   - Educate the patient and any available family members about the potential causes of symptoms.
   - Instruct on recognizing signs of worsening neurological status or cognitive impairment, emphasizing the importance of timely follow-up.

6. **Follow-Up**
   - Schedule follow-up appointments to reassess neurological symptoms, cognitive function, and response to any new treatments initiated.
   - Establish a timeline for follow-up with the neurologist and ophthalmologist to ensure continuous evaluation of patient status.
