## Assessment
1. **Blurred Vision and Decreased Communication**: Likely due to diabetic retinopathy, considering the patient's history of diabetes (metformin use) and associated visual symptoms. Needs further evaluation with a comprehensive eye examination.
  
2. **Confusion and Decreased Judgment**: Possible neurological condition such as dementia or acute changes due to a stroke. Immediate neuroimaging (CT or MRI) is warranted to rule out structural causes.

3. **Tingling in Hands and Feet**: Most likely associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy secondary to long-standing diabetes. Other potential causes such as vitamin B12 deficiency need to be ruled out.

4. **Medical History**: Multiple comorbidities including gingival disease, alveolitis, and prior fractures which may complicate treatment options.

## Plan
1. **Immediate Investigations**:
   - Obtain **CT or MRI of the brain** to evaluate for any acute cerebral events or structural changes contributing to confusion.
   - Conduct **fundoscopic examination** to assess for signs of diabetic retinopathy. Consider **OCT** to detail retinal changes.
   - Check **Vitamin B12 levels** and **glucose levels** to rule out deficiencies and assess diabetic control.
   - Perform **Thyroid Function Tests** to evaluate for hypothyroidism.

2. **Medication Review**:
   - Review the current medication regimen for any drugs contributing to cognitive impairment, particularly sedatives or opioids like meperidine. Consider switching to alternatives if necessary.
   - Ensure that the patient is compliant with diabetic management medications (metformin, lisinopril, hydrochlorothiazide).

3. **Non-Pharmacological Interventions**:
   - Initiate **cognitive therapy** focused on problem-solving and memory improvement strategies.
   - Encourage **social engagement** through group activities to promote cognitive stimulation.
   - Implement a **structured daily routine** to enhance orientation and reduce confusion.

4. **Follow-Up**:
   - Schedule follow-up appointments for neurologist evaluation after imaging results.
   - Arrange for an ophthalmology referral for comprehensive eye examination.
   - Plan regular follow-ups to monitor glucose control and overall health management.

5. **Patient Education**:
   - Educate the patient and caregivers about the potential impact of diabetes on cognitive function and vision.
   - Discuss the importance of adherence to prescribed treatments and lifestyle changes to manage diabetes effectively. 

6. **Consideration of Home Health Services**: Given the patient’s confusion and cognitive impairment, explore the option of home health assistance to ensure safety and support with daily activities.
